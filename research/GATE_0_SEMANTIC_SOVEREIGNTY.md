> **A Stream Collision occurs when computational streams governed by distinct control domains become mutually consequential across a governance boundary.**

## Admission test

1. Distinct governance
2. Boundary crossing
3. Mutual consequence

## Exclusions

Not admitted by default:
- an internal pipeline under one authority;
- a physical interconnect alone;
- a chiplet-to-chiplet link alone;
- a CPU/GPU/QPU handoff under one single governing system;
- a memory or photonic interface merely because data crosses it;
- established failures or effects that already have stable engineering names.

## Negative control

OpenQSE/LLNL material distinguishes remote quantum resources from local owned/controlled resources. A remote QPU can create a second queue that the scheduler cannot see or control; a local resource under one owned/controlled queue does not create the same condition.

## First proving domain

**HPC workload-management domain ↔ remote quantum-resource/provider domain**

This remains the only authorized proving case until mandatory re-evaluation.

## Advanced-compute context

Advanced chips are now part of the strategic context. UCIe 3.0 is relevant because scalable multi-chip systems increasingly require manageability beyond raw transport, including firmware management, priority signaling, runtime recalibration, fast throttle and emergency notifications.

These features show increasing coordination and manageability. They do not, by themselves, prove independent governance.
